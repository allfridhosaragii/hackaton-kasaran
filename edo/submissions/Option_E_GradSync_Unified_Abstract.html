Abstract

1. Problem. Across their university lifecycle, undergraduates face four connected bottlenecks caused by rigid legacy portals and fragmented chat groups: (a) during course registration (KRS/FRS), students lack real syllabus previews, face server slowdowns, and get separated from their study circle; (b) in lectures and group projects (Tugas Besar), fast-paced slides hinder comprehension while lecturers cannot track individual progress or prevent free-riders; (c) in semesters 5–7, scholarship and MoU-internship posters get buried in chat groups, delaying credit (SKS) conversion and alumni networking; and (d) during thesis (Tugas Akhir) supervision, unstated advisor quotas, messaging anxiety, and extended advisor inactivity stall graduation.

2. Intended Users. First- through final-year undergraduate students, course lecturers supervising team assignments, and program coordinators (Kaprodi/Dosen Wali) overseeing credit conversions and thesis advisor workloads.

3. Supporting Observations or Evidence. From multi-campus workflow audits and preliminary interviews across ITB, Telkom University Surabaya, and UNESA [1], students identified stalled thesis revisions from overloaded advisors as their top graduation bottleneck, compounded by untransparent advisor quotas and WhatsApp messaging anxiety. Respondents also confirmed frequent free-riding in lecturer-assigned group projects (currently tracked via manual sheets), buried scholarship/internship posters in student association chats, and registration quota clashes.

4. Proposed Solution and Core User Flow. AcademiaFlow is a semester-adaptive web workspace using one-click KRS PDF import: (1) Smart KRS & Circle Sync: previews 14-week syllabi, practicum schedules, and lecturer styles while simulating conflict-free parallel classes with live quota indicators; (2) StudyPulse & Accountable Groupwork: students drop lecture audio and slide PDFs to generate slide-synced transcripts, Socratic explanations, FSRS active-recall quizzes, and shared notes, while team boards auto-intersect free hours, trigger relay handover alerts when a prerequisite task finishes, stream contribution logs to lecturers, and allow objective free-rider escalation; (3) Adaptive Opportunity & Alumni Hub (Semesters 5–7): surfaces filtered scholarships, MoU internships with 20-SKS conversion simulators, activity-point (TAK/SKKM) trackers, and opt-in alumni contacts (WA/IG/LinkedIn); and (4) Thesis SLA & In-App Supervision: provides semantic advisor matching, standardized consultation tickets with one-click logbook approval, and 21-day inactivity alerts recommending replacement advisors to coordinators.

5. How It Differs from Existing Alternatives. Legacy portals (SIX, iGracias, SIAKAD) only record registration and final grades, whereas generic chat, note, and kanban apps lack KRS schedule intersection, slide-synced audio active recall, lecturer contribution telemetry, MoU SKS mapping, and advisor SLA rebalancing. AcademiaFlow unifies these workflows without requiring write-access to university databases.

6. Planned Technologies. A Next.js 15 and TypeScript web app backed by Supabase (PostgreSQL, pgvector, Auth, and Realtime WebSockets). Collaborative notes and quizzes use BlockNote, Yjs, and ts-fsrs, while a Python FastAPI service uses faster-whisper, MarkItDown, PaddleOCR, and Google LangExtract (Gemini API) to parse audio, KRS/syllabus PDFs, and posters.

7. Expected Benefit and Development Plan. Expected benefit: cut exam review and group coordination time by 50%, curb free-riding, and speed up SKS consultation and logbook approval in a two-week trial with 30 students. Plan: Week 1 KRS Circle Sync and StudyPulse/Groupwork Engine; Week 2 Opportunity/Alumni Hub and Thesis SLA Rebalancer; Week 3 pilot testing. Unavailable campus APIs are mitigated via PDF parsing.

Source Acknowledgments. [1] Multi-campus student workflow audit and field interviews across Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB), Telkom University Surabaya, and Universitas Negeri Surabaya (UNESA) (November 2026).
